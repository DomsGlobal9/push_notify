Sending happens in the background so your backend gets an answer instantly. The queue holds that background work as jobs. When things are healthy this list is almost always empty: jobs are deleted the moment they finish.

Two kinds of job

KindWhat it does
expandWorks out who a notification goes to, 500 devices at a time, and creates one send_batch per 500. Scheduled sends wait here until their time.
send_batchSends to up to 500 devices at once through Google/Apple and records the results. Phones with temporary errors go into a new send_batch that runs a little later (a retry).

Statuses

  • pending waiting for its time (retries and scheduled sends wait on purpose).
  • running being worked on now. If the server restarts mid-job, the job goes back to pending automatically after 10 minutes.
  • failed crashed 5 times in a row and was given up on. Its notification still completes; the affected phones count as failed. Last error says why.

When something looks stuck

  1. Check the Overview's Queue card. Falling behind during a big broadcast is normal for a minute or two.
  2. Many pending jobs with times in the past and nothing running: the server was asleep or restarting. On the free Render plan make sure the uptime monitor pings /healthz every 5 minutes.
  3. A failed job: read Last error. Fix the cause (usually a credential), then select the job → action Requeue selected jobs now → Go.
warning
Do not delete pending jobs to "clear" the queue: that drops the pushes they would have sent. To stop a send, cancel the notification instead (your backend: POST /v1/notifications/<id>/cancel).

Failed jobs are cleaned up automatically after 7 days.
